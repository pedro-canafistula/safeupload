# Independent review: WinFsp writer-only owner plan

Source and architecture review, 2026-10-06. This note reviews the frozen
writer-only plan and its referenced filesystem-owner, feasibility, and
protocol/journal notes. It does not modify product source, tests, the frozen
plan, VM state, signing state, or dependency installation. No implementation
or runtime privacy qualification is claimed.

## Verdict

The writer-only filesystem-owner direction is a plausible bounded proof route
for the local fixed-NTFS mapping problem. It avoids extending the current
minifilter-created application SOP, gives the writer stream to a real FSD,
and leaves independent public readers and raw observers on the original NTFS
file. The frozen plan is **not ready to implement as written**: its M2
generation-boundary clause conflicts with the now-approved whole-owner-
generation interpretation, and several kernel-to-service, identity, and
last-close claims remain explicit proof gates. Do not treat a local WinFsp
prototype as closure of the all-destinations MVP.

## Material correction: keep one private generation through policy expansion

The frozen plan says that a retained section remains on its original private
generation, but then requires post-boundary writes to enter a successor
generation before an approved commit. That is internally inconsistent and
unnecessary for the accepted M2 case. Keep one immutable owner generation
private for the complete lifetime of its WinFsp FileNode, including writes
through a retained section before and after policy expansion. The generation
identity and owner binding stay fixed while authorized writes may change its
private contents. Policy activation does not commit, rebind, or split that
generation. Once the entire owner
generation is quiescent, the service may durably flush and seal that one
generation; only an exact identity/scope/active-policy match can then enter
the existing inspection, approval, and publication flow. If any mapped view,
cache write-behind, FSP request, or backing write remains live, keep it private
and unsealed.

Pinned WinFsp `src/sys/create.c` reuses the existing FileNode for the same
internal name, and the API describes this as the same file-node context while
that name is open. That supports one stable FSP name/SOP per owner generation
and distinct names for distinct concurrent owners. It does not justify
creating a successor for a policy change. The M2 acceptance case should retain
the same name, FileNode, and backing generation over expansion, then verify
successful post-expansion mapped write and flush while the public NTFS bytes
and extents remain unchanged. The full accumulated private generation becomes
eligible for inspection only after the owner has drained and the policy
generation matches.

The old M1 timing is also superseded by the approved early-protection rule:
the historical repro opens/maps before filter attach, while the accepted
design must have the owner ready before the first application writable open.
Change only that trigger in a future test version. Preserve the meaningful
privacy predicates: successful mapped write and `FlushViewOfFile`, successful
section/view disposal and `FlushFileBuffers`, successful direct-NTFS buffered
and uncached reads, stable full raw extent bytes and allocation, and all 20
unchanged raw samples. Keep the old repro's attach-gap sequence as historical
evidence; do not run it as the new owner acceptance test. The current scripts'
“independent” destination reads open fresh file objects but execute in the
same PowerShell process; the new proof should use distinct observer processes
and report their image and process identities. Keep the raw observer separate
from the writer process as well.

## Handoff and authorization boundary

Microsoft's documented ECP APIs make the proposed carrier plausible: a
minifilter can attach a driver-defined ECP list to a create, and dynamically
added ECPs are propagated through create reparses. This does not prove the
exact source-filter-to-WinFsp stack, nor does it give the WinFsp user-mode
Create/Open callback an ECP parameter. The safe boundary is: a minifilter
instance on the WinFsp target volume validates the kernel-created ECP and
consumes the matching one-use grant before the FSD sees the create; the
service uses an authenticated prepared grant record and internal generation
name as lookup data, never as authority. The service must default-deny direct
private-name opens and must not fall back to PID/path-only authorization.

Before implementation qualification, test the exact reparse stack: the target
volume filter receives the expected ECP after reparse; it is kernel-originated;
requestor process-object and source identity match; and the grant is consumed
exactly once. Direct FSP path, user-forged ECP, stale/replayed grant, different
process, changed file/parent identity, service restart, absent filter instance,
and grant-table loss must all deny the create. Confirm preservation of other
create-semantic ECPs. The ECP only authorizes the create handoff; subsequent
data I/O must remain attached to the prepared FileNode/generation and its
service-side authorization table. ECPs are not credentials, and the WinFsp
service callback must not be described as reading one.

## Lifetime, identity, and access gates

The pinned WinFsp `file.c` deliberately distinguishes Cleanup from Close and
retains a FileNode so mapped views or write-behind can reuse it. Its mmap test
can close a user file handle before writing through a mapped view. The pinned
noncached test waits for overlapped writes before closing; it does not exercise
pending completion across last close. These upstream results support retaining
the node/backing past Cleanup, but they do not prove that a Close callback alone
means every view, Cache Manager write-behind, paging operation, FSP transaction,
or backing write has drained. The proof must correlate final FSD close, section
and cache lifetime, outstanding requests/completions, flush result, and the
service's backing `FlushFileBuffers` before sealing. Cleanup/close notifications
remain hints until that exact lifecycle is demonstrated.

The writer-only route changes the writer's filesystem object/device. The
current M1/M2 scripts compare the original NTFS destination and raw LCN/bytes;
they do not assert that the writer handle's lower `DEVICE_OBJECT` equals the
public reader's NTFS object. Therefore same physical writer-device identity is
an implementation preference unless another canonical test says otherwise.
That does not relax application-visible logical name, access, volume/file-ID
queries that the product currently supports, aliases, ADS, rename/replacement,
share modes, or publication identity. Pin current support/refusal for
`FILE_OPEN_BY_FILE_ID`, hard links, and other WinFsp gaps before claiming no
scope reduction: preserve any already-supported behavior; retain a refusal
only where the current product already refuses that operation. Test the
original token/DACL decision, generic access mapping, direct-path denial,
handle inheritance/duplication, and owner/service/filter failure. A configurable
WinFsp volume serial or 64-bit index is not proof of the original NTFS full
file identity.

## Required qualification gates and decision boundary

1. Resolve the exact filesystem-owner package/version and license before
   adding it. The plan's v2.2B4 commit is a prerelease source pin; it is not a
   product-qualified dependency. No SDK, link target, FSP project, service, or
   attachment path exists in the pinned SafeUpload snapshot. The source study
   and empty VM inventories are not runtime coverage.
2. Prove owner readiness and early admission on a clean boot before any
   candidate application write-capable open. Load the complete immutable
   candidate-scope superset without scanning files or directory contents.
   Every destination scope entry, mount, allocator, journal, and recovery state
   must be complete. Missing state refuses a candidate mutation; it may never
   fall through to public NTFS. Keep policy inspection/publication delayed as
   authorized. A local proof does not produce all-destination Ready.
3. Prove one-owner same-name/same-FileNode behavior and two-owner distinct
   FileNodes; same-writer read-your-writes remains private. Run M1/M2 on actual
   NTFS with independent observer processes and a separate raw-volume process.
   Require successful write/flush/disposal, exact full-byte and extent
   comparisons, stable allocation, and all 20 unchanged samples. Any refused
   operation or missing observer is inconclusive, not a privacy pass.
4. Prove exact source identity and namespace compatibility: original normalized
   names, parent/file identity, supported ID opens and queries, ADS, aliases,
   rename/replacement, delete, sharing, and open-by-ID where currently
   supported. A name-to-object revalidation race, unknown identity, unsupported
   behavior gap, or mismatched target leaves the generation private.
5. Prove generation durability and recovery under owner/service/filter stop,
   forced process stop, reboot, journal replay, and policy update. Only the
   entire matching private generation may be promoted after real final
   quiescence and flush. No inspection, approval, permit, or publication is
   allowed while it is `OwnerPendingPolicy` or while lifetime is uncertain.
6. Keep taint, staging default-off, and all unsupported-capability controls
   unchanged until the canonical DOD passes: the other supported destination
   rows, USB removal/reinsert, UNC/SMB server-side boundary, real sync client,
   recovery, Verifier, stress, latency, required builds/tests, and independent
   restoration. A client-side local redirect is not proof for a remote writer.
