# Independent review: T5 libvirt drain and active-guest recovery design

**Disposition: NO-GO for implementation approval, host/service changes, libvirt operations, or product VM use.** The terminal-fence architecture is conditionally plausible: stopping the exact old `libvirtd` process can end its in-process request handlers without relying on thread-pool counters or client-close behavior, and `KillMode=process` does not itself require killing QEMU guests. The frozen design is not yet a complete recovery protocol. It has a wrong lease-restart handoff, an unspecified listener gate after daemon restart, and unresolved process/helper/job and source-baseline proofs. These gaps do not authorize a daemon restart, guest operation, path migration, or policy change.

## Findings

**P1 — The guardian-restart handoff is not sound as written.** Step 7 says to acquire new owner-bound leases on newly opened descriptors, store them, and then release the old descriptor-store references. Root's pinned preliminary-findings JSON reports a pending-break control in which a fresh-descriptor `F_SETLEASE` returns `EAGAIN` while the old same-open-file-description lease is break-pending. That JSON does not contain the raw control bytes or output in this eight-file design bundle, so I treat the `EAGAIN` result as a root finding that must be independently pinned before implementation, not as a control I reproduced. A2's separately pinned owner-exit controls establish that a descriptor-store duplicate can retain a lease after the setter exits, that `/proc/locks` visibility can disappear when the owner PID exits, and that `F_GETLEASE` may return `F_UNLCK` as the pending break target while a writer remains blocked. Thus an absent `/proc/locks` row or `F_GETLEASE == F_UNLCK` must not be interpreted as lease loss or write admission, and retrying `F_SETLEASE` is not an ownership-transfer protocol.

The same-open-file-description `F_SETSIG`/`F_SETOWN_EX` retarget path is the relevant candidate design, but the evidence in the captured A2 review does not qualify it through deployed systemd restarts, manager restart, or every pending-break timing. The design must specify and qualify reattachment on the *same* stored descriptor, verify the signal owner readback, parse `ACTIVE` versus `BREAKING` and orphan owner rows under the right PID namespace, and say exactly what happens on a pending break. A pending break must stay nonterminal and fail closed until a reviewed recovery path resolves it. The fresh-open sequence in the frozen design is not an acceptable substitute. If guardian or manager loss can remove the last retained descriptor, the design has no proven continuous-custody path.

**P1 — Restarting `libvirtd` reopens the listener gate unless a recovery-only admission mechanism is specified.** The pinned vendor unit has `Wants=` for `libvirtd.socket`, `libvirtd-ro.socket`, and `libvirtd-admin.socket`; its `Also=` install links also include those sockets. Starting `libvirtd.service` therefore can start the legacy sockets again. The design says that the restarted daemon may accept only recovery traffic while `PREPARING` remains closed, but the canonical flock and an ordinary requester gate are not shown to intercept all libvirt clients or all domains. The newer generation receipt also records a deployed service drop-in whose contents were withheld; its effective behavior is not part of the unit-file pin. The design must provide a concrete way to keep all external endpoints closed or enforce a reviewed recovery-only policy across every endpoint while QEMU is reattached. Otherwise newly admitted requests can mutate the selected domain, cold alias, pools, or source paths during recovery.

The observer-scoped listener capture found three active legacy sockets and no matching `16509`/`16514` listener in its observed network namespace, with proxy/modular units inactive. It explicitly does not prove host-wide listener closure: the observer has its own PID, mount, and network namespace IDs, cannot inspect `/proc/1/ns/*`, and `ss` results are namespace-limited. The receipt also says the apparent absence of `/etc/libvirt/hooks` is only for that observer filesystem; it is not proof that deployed hooks are absent. All proxy read-only/admin/TCP/TLS sockets, modular sockets, direct config listeners, and any session instance still need authoritative, host-scoped review.

**P1 — The pidfd fence identity and survivor policy are not established.** The generation receipt stably observed `libvirtd` PID 1205, start ticks 2145, and cgroup `/system.slice/libvirtd.service`, but access to `/proc/1205/exe`, its namespace links, and its FD directory was denied. The listener receipt's observer has `NSpid=1562778`, while reads of `/proc/1/ns/*` were denied. A systemd `MainPID` returned in the manager's PID namespace and a PID passed to `pidfd_open()` in the guardian's namespace are not proven to identify the same task here. The implementation needs to pin namespace mapping (`NSpid`), exact PID/start-time, unit invocation ID, executable identity and pidfd target in one verified namespace, and fail closed on mismatch. A stable numeric PID alone is insufficient.

The captured cgroup member list is `1205, 1580, 1581`. Root's later read-only process observation identified 1580/1581 as `dnsmasq` processes; that short `ps` output was not separately hash-pinned. A rule requiring every non-QEMU member of the cgroup to exit would therefore either stall while service-owned networking remains active or tempt an unsafe kill that disrupts unrelated networking/guests. Keep the old-daemon process/thread group fence distinct from child classification: explicitly allow only attributed infrastructure proved not to hold protected files or continue relevant mutations. Do not kill unclassified helpers, DNS services, or unrelated QEMU guests. Conversely, `KillMode=process` and main-PID exit alone do not prove that helper side effects or file handles have ended.

**P1 — The QEMU-reconciliation step is directionally right but does not yet prove coordinator-loss recovery.** The design correctly rejects pool counters, client close, and `prioWorkers=0` as barriers; the installed header is declarations only, and the documented async job distinction is relevant. It also correctly intends to leave QEMU running, use a private overlay/state, inspect jobs, and require orderly shutdown before restoring XML. But the frozen evidence has no host-scoped proof of the selected QEMU process identity/cgroup/FDS, no complete helper tree, no active-job inventory, and no hook inventory. The new receipts expressly record permission-denied executable/FD/namespace observations and no drain. A daemon reconnect may invoke a QEMU hook; the design itself recognizes that a failing hook can kill a running guest, but the exact deployed hooks/configuration and behavior are unreviewed.

Before any baseline or cleanup, the recovery executor needs an exact inventory/reconciliation rule for all jobs and delegated helpers that can touch the selected disk chain, NVRAM, TPM, or run overlay. It must distinguish a QEMU job that safely continues on a private run overlay from one that can still write a baseline or alter persistent XML/pool state. Wait for or reconcile every target operation before baseline and before restore; treat unknown jobs or partial effects as retained quarantine. Preexisting unrelated long jobs must be terminal before the global restart or the fence is deferred. These requirements can preserve unrelated guests only if the hook and job evidence proves that outcome; stopping the daemon is not itself proof of it.

**P1 — Full baseline restoration still depends on an unreviewed selected path migration and an underspecified metadata proof.** The A2 review records that the selected `/mnt/storage` path has requester-writable ancestors and fails its strict path preflight. The design rightly says read leases pin the inode, not the pathname's parent directory entries, and proposes a root-controlled copy of the full chain plus persistent state. That is a concrete alternative to the rejected path, not a proof that a safe destination, capacity, QEMU access, or complete object/reference inventory exists. The design correctly treats rebinding both `win10-debug3` and the exact cold alias `win10-debug2` as a selected VM/storage migration needing review. Keep that work path-specific and owner-reviewed; do not generalize it into a universal owner-permission rule.

The current receipt has no complete root-scoped chain/pool/reference inventory, no destination capacity/filesystem proof, and no complete NVRAM/TPM/managed-save/snapshot/file-backed-state inventory. A full-stream copy check alone does not prove initial dirty-page drain or preserve all required metadata. The design should explicitly require the accepted source identity, same-descriptor `fsync`/read-lease verification, no writable FD/shared mapping at acquisition, exact before/after directory-entry identity, and a defined metadata comparison set including ownership/mode, ACLs, xattrs/SELinux labels, hard links, file and parent-directory membership, and TPM directory contents. Final verification must compare the restored *original paths/objects* and persistent XML/state to the pre-run checkpoint using a separate verifier; the fact that a protected duplicate matches a checkpoint is not by itself proof that the original path and metadata were restored. Retain the private duplicate until that independent receipt passes.

**P1 — The failure state is safe but does not meet the required terminal outcome.** “Quarantine and retain everything” is an appropriate response to unknown state, lease break, hook failure, or nonterminal job. It is not an executable recovery-to-baseline path. The design's stated objective is independent baseline restoration after coordinator loss, yet a guardian restart with a pending break or an unresolved job can stop permanently in quarantine. Before product execution, implement a reviewed recovery executor for each persisted mutation phase, including how it restores XML/state after an ambiguous API result and what the operator can safely do if the systemd manager or host restarts. If some failures necessarily remain quarantined, product-run qualification must say that those states violate the required completion objective and remain disallowed.

**P2 — Correct the source-review digest in the design.** The malformed A2 report digest prevents an exact comparison as printed. The root freeze captured the correct digest separately, so this is a repairable evidence-pin error, not evidence that the A2 report itself changed. Keep the frozen design immutable; use a revised design snapshot with the corrected 64-character digest.

## Shortest path to a reviewable, full-scope implementation

1. Revise the design snapshot to correct the A2 pin, replace the fresh-descriptor restart handoff with same-descriptor custody and explicit pending-break semantics, and distinguish the dead daemon generation from safe persistent child infrastructure. Specify a terminal state for guardian loss, systemd-manager restart, and host reboot.
2. Obtain authoritative read-only captures of effective systemd units/drop-ins without disclosing secrets, every endpoint/activator in the host namespaces, PID namespace mapping, exact old-daemon and helper identities/FDS, QEMU PIDs/cgroups, hook files/config, active jobs, polkit identities/rules, and the selected disk-chain/pool/alias/NVRAM/TPM/other-state inventory. The present receipts are bounded observations, not host-wide closure or generation custody proof.
3. Define a recovery-only way to restart/reconnect libvirt while all ordinary mutation paths remain gated. Pin its exact endpoint and authorization behavior. If the vendor unit's `Wants=` sockets must be changed or a temporary policy/drop-in added, present that concrete, reversible host transaction for review and owner approval; this design does not authorize it.
4. Review a concrete protected-path transaction for the selected chain and exact cold alias: chosen destination, full capacity, metadata-preserving copy, QEMU access, exact XML/pool-reference update and rollback, and independent restoration of original paths and persistent state. The user-writable `/mnt/storage` ancestor fact is specific to this selected path.
5. Implement the guardian/coordinator split and journaled recovery executor. Test on a disposable domain with the pinned daemon/service/filesystem/lease setup: each API mutation boundary, coordinator loss with QEMU live, same-FD reattach, pending break, partial helper effect, active QEMU job, service restart, hook success/failure, and independent full-stream plus metadata restoration. Keep unrelated guests alive throughout. Manager-restart and host-loss recovery require explicit qualification or an explicit product-use boundary.

Until these steps pass an independent review, the exact architecture remains conditional and the frozen design remains **NO-GO**. No design approval, source integration, runtime permission, owner approval, or migration authorization is inferred here.
