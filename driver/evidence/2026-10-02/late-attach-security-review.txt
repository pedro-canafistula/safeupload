Reviewed baseline

Pinned source under review: commit 5b233d53d2508d4c1aa168a7ce765e531d2d5bf0.
The review covered the complete StageFence.c, Filter.c, Filter.h, Protocol.h,

Findings in the pinned source
-----------------------------
1. SafeUploadStageFencePrepareUnload returned STATUS_SUCCESS immediately when
   FenceEntryCount was zero. A late attach could queue a refresh while the table
   was empty; the unload callback could therefore accept unload before the scan
   found an already mapped stream.
2. FenceLateRefreshRoutine cleared FenceLateRefreshPending at callback entry,
   before acquiring the refresh mutex or scanning. The pending flag therefore
   did not represent queued or active work, and another arrival during the scan
   could be coalesced without guaranteeing another pass.
3. Queue allocation/queue failures were ignored by InstanceSetup, which still
   returned STATUS_SUCCESS. The worker discarded the refresh NTSTATUS. The
   Inspector's complete result ignored outstanding work and failure state.
4. FltUnregisterFilter waits for generic work-item rundown only after the
   FilterUnload decision; that rundown cannot repair the earlier unload guard.

Microsoft contracts checked
---------------------------
- PFLT_INSTANCE_SETUP_CALLBACK documents that STATUS_FLT_DO_NOT_ATTACH declines
  the offered attachment and cautions against synchronization in the callback:
  https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows-hardware/drivers/ddi/fltkernel/nc-fltkernel-pflt_instance_setup_callback
- FltQueueGenericWorkItem runs its callback in a system worker thread and can
  fail if the filter is deleting:
  https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows-hardware/drivers/ddi/fltkernel/nf-fltkernel-fltqueuegenericworkitem
- FltUnregisterFilter drains generic work items during unregister, after the
  FilterUnload callback has already returned:
  https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows-hardware/drivers/ifs/unregistering-the-minifilter
- STATUS_FLT_DO_NOT_DETACH prevents a voluntary unload; mandatory unload cannot
  be refused:
  https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows-hardware/drivers/ddi/fltkernel/nc-fltkernel-pflt_filter_unload_callback
- FltGetVolumeName is callable at <= APC_LEVEL and supplies a volume name when
  called from the PASSIVE_LEVEL refresh worker:
  https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows-hardware/drivers/ddi/fltkernel/nf-fltkernel-fltgetvolumename
- FltAllocateGenericWorkItem, FltQueueGenericWorkItem, and FltFreeGenericWorkItem
  are callable through DISPATCH_LEVEL; the queued callback runs at PASSIVE_LEVEL:
  https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows-hardware/drivers/ddi/fltkernel/nf-fltkernel-fltallocategenericworkitem
  https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows-hardware/drivers/ddi/fltkernel/nf-fltkernel-fltqueuegenericworkitem
- KeFlushQueuedDpcs waits for queued DPCs, and must run at PASSIVE_LEVEL:
  https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows-hardware/drivers/ddi/wdm/nf-wdm-keflushqueueddpcs

Implemented follow-ups in the working tree
------------------------------------------
- Owner policy decision (approved): use the commercial-DLP behavior: fail closed
  on the protected scope, fail open elsewhere, retry transient scan failures,
  and report quarantine/retry state.
- A packed interlocked admission state combines the unload gate with an
  outstanding-work count. InstanceSetup never waits on that gate: it reserves
  and queues one refresh per offered attachment, or immediately returns
  STATUS_FLT_DO_NOT_ATTACH. Counting each accepted item avoids losing arrivals
  after an earlier scan enumerated volumes. The final unload scan always runs,
  even with an empty table.
- The gate remains closed through both the fence and staged-stream unload
  checks. Filter.c commits it only after the full staged prepare succeeds and
  reopens it if a later guard refuses a voluntary unload. Mandatory unload
  commits teardown even when the guard reports retained protected state.
- The refresh work item retains the offered volume. Failed scans quarantine the
  captured failing volume and late-attach trigger by referenced PFLT_VOLUME
  identity when the volume is fixed local NTFS and therefore within scan
  coverage. A volume-name lookup failure is recorded against that volume and
  does not set global quarantine. Global quarantine is used only when
  FltEnumerateVolumes fails or reports more than the supported volume bound.
  In protected-name create paths, Objects->Volume scopes volume quarantine;
  fenced names are checked against the installed name table. A whole-volume
  policy prefix correctly makes every name on that volume protected. File-ID
  opens and resource-failed name queries remain refused on a volume with fence
  entries or quarantine.
- Quarantine does not deny paging writes. Paging writes are denied only for
  streams in the installed fence table; unrelated files remain available. This
  is the owner's fail-closed protected-scope, fail-open elsewhere policy.
  Quarantine contributes to HasEntries and blocks voluntary unload.
- A KTIMER/KDPC retry uses exponential backoff from one second to a 60-second
  cap. The DPC only reserves and queues a Filter Manager generic work item;
  scanning runs at PASSIVE_LEVEL. StateFlags reports RETRY_PENDING without
  changing SAFEUPLOAD_FENCE_STATUS size or protocol identity. A successful scan
  with no skipped scopes releases quarantine only for volumes it enumerated and
  scanned; global quarantine clears only after successful enumeration. Pruning
  still releases references for volumes no longer enumerated.
- Voluntary prepare closes admission, sets the retry stop state, cancels the
  timer, flushes queued DPCs, drains accepted generic work, then performs its
  synchronous final scan. A parked retry owns no late-work unit, so unload
  never waits on a sleeping timer. If voluntary unload is cancelled while
  quarantine remains, reopening the gate schedules the retry again. Mandatory
  teardown repeats timer cancellation and DPC flushing; FltUnregisterFilter
  drains generic work items before StageFence state is freed.
- Setup admission now precedes instance-context allocation, so a failed queue
  returns DO_NOT_ATTACH without relying on cleanup of a context installed by a
  setup callback that later declines attachment.
- The existing status Reserved word is now StateFlags without changing the
  struct size or protocol identity. Inspector complete is false while work,
  the unload gate, quarantine, skipped scope, or last scan failure remains.

Round-2 fixes (2026-10-03)
-------------------------
- Restored the normalized-name lookup against FenceTable->Names at both create
  gates, combined with the quarantined-volume check. A fenced protected name is
  refused after a successful scan; paging-write denial remains limited to
  registered fenced streams.
- Checked FltObjectReference before queueing late-attach work. A failed
  reference releases the reserved work unit and work item, records the failure,
  and declines attachment. The worker dereferences only a non-NULL trigger
  volume reference.
- Added the target-volume quarantine check to permit-based publication rename.
  A quarantined target is refused with STATUS_SHARING_VIOLATION and counted as
  an open refusal. The service's read-only open path is unchanged.
- Limited volume quarantine to fixed local NTFS, using the collector's
  predicate. Unsupported triggers are traced and not quarantined. Successful
  no-skipped-scope scans release only quarantined volumes present in that
  scan's enumerated/scanned set; enumeration failure cannot clear global
  quarantine. The collector still prunes unmounted volumes.
- Documented why pre-enumeration failures without a trigger preserve proven
  state: startup fails before filtering, policy expansion refuses the swap on
  a failed pre-swap scan, the post-swap scan retains the pre-swap union, and
  explicit refresh/retry keeps the prior fence or quarantine.
- Corrected the DPC routine declaration and pageable placement: refresh helpers
  and their spin-lock helpers remain nonpaged where required, and retry startup
  is no longer placed in PAGE while acquiring FenceRetryLock.
- No round-2 WDK build or VM result is claimed here.

Open items found by independent review (pre-existing at HEAD, out of this increment)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Reads through handles opened before quarantine or a scan; the current gates
  run at create time only.
- A read-only hard-link alias outside the protected prefix.
- Alternate data stream mappings are not enumerated by the scan.
- The service's read-only protected opens are not refused by the quarantine
  path.
- Reparse points skipped by the scan do not make `complete` false.
- The instance-detach check-then-act window.
- `complete` is a sampled status, not a synchronized snapshot.
- Protected-scope files modified while quarantined are not re-inspected after
  recovery.

Explicit limits and remaining gates
-----------------------------------
This change does not close the interval from an accepted/actual attachment to
completion of its scan. A paging write in that interval remains an open race;
the code and harness must not be represented as proving admission closure.
The quarantine protects opens of protected names after a failed scan is
detected, not retroactively for I/O that completed before failure detection.
Paging writeback is not blocked merely because a volume is quarantined, so
dirty bytes from a protected mapping can reach disk while quarantine is active.
Per-volume quarantine is released only after a successful scan with no skipped
scopes and only for quarantined volumes enumerated and scanned; global
quarantine requires successful enumeration as well.
Files in the protected scope modified while quarantined are not re-inspected
after recovery; this is out of this increment and needs a taint/re-inspection
decision. Removable and network scopes, pre-existing handles that create later
mappings, alternate data streams, hard-link aliases, and data protection after
mandatory filter loss remain outside this slice. Fixed local NTFS volumes are
the scan's supported volume scope.

The VM outputs already recorded before this source change are historical and
are not fresh evidence for this implementation. The new harness is source-only
until the parent builds/packages it and runs the pinned old/fixed A/B on the
isolated debuggee. No VM, remote build, commit, or push was performed here.

Round-3 source follow-up (2026-10-03; not independently reviewed or built)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
This section records the previous source snapshot. Its queue-failure and snapshot-pruning
statements are superseded by Round 4 below.
- Manual QueryTeardown now refuses when the target volume has any ordinary
  fence entry or quarantine and when the packed admission count, refresh-active
  count, unload gate, or retry-pending state says work is pending or active.
  The public Inspector refresh and policy transition reserve admission before
  scanning; new reservations are rejected after unload closes the gate, work
  reserved before closure is drained, and the unload's final scan calls the
  private scanner after that drain. Policy pending state is not published when
  its transition reservation fails.
- The manual-detach check is still point-in-time. A scan/late attach can be
  admitted after QueryTeardown returns SUCCESS but before Filter Manager
  completes detach. This remains a cutover blocker. A closure needs a per-volume
  DETACHING reservation coordinated with scan and attach admission and validated
  teardown start/complete (including rollback) callbacks; the current callback
  table has no such lifecycle guard.
- Supported fixed local NTFS late attachments still require a successfully
  referenced and queued refresh; allocation, reference, queue, or closed-gate
  failure declines that attachment. Out-of-coverage network, removable, and
  non-NTFS attachments now skip fence work with a trace and remain attached so
  a transient fence queue failure cannot disable the normal policy filter.
  The FltMgr InstanceSetup contract cautions against thread synchronization or
  IPC there even at PASSIVE_LEVEL, so this code does not wait for its queued
  scan. The post-attach/pre-scan interval remains an explicit cutover blocker.
  The safe next design must gate volume usability outside InstanceSetup or
  obtain a scoped per-file pre-attach mapping inventory; it must not deny all
  paging writes on a volume.
- A failure after fixed-NTFS enumeration now quarantines every enumerated volume
  plus the late-attach trigger, including a no-trigger Inspector refresh whose
  carried-over table build exceeds the 64-stream limit. This closes the
  missing-quarantine path for opens and unload, but the failed build discards
  partial stream entries. Quarantine does not make paging writes consult the
  missing table entry. A 65-mapping marker can therefore reach public bytes;
  this is a blocking privacy/cutover residual under the approved no-volume-wide
  paging-denial policy. Do not call the capacity case fail-closed for bytes.
- Before installing a successful generation for a late-attach worker, the scan
  now confirms that its trigger volume identity is in the enumerated fixed-NTFS
  snapshot. If Filter Manager's changing global volume list omits it, the scan
  fails with STATUS_DEVICE_BUSY, quarantines the snapshot and trigger, and lets
  automatic retry take a later snapshot. This prevents a false success that
  silently omits the trigger's mappings; it does not close the pre-scan write
  window.
- **Unresolved P2 lifetime issue:** `FENCE_TABLE.StreamVolume` stores raw
  `PFLT_VOLUME` identity values without references. `FenceReleaseVolumes`
  releases the `FltEnumerateVolumes` references after the scan while the table
  remains installed; later entry and teardown checks compare the stored value.
  Pointer reuse could create a false volume match or miss. Do not rely on the
  per-volume entry check as stable until ownership is fixed. A candidate design
  is a table-owned referenced volume set with explicit transfer/reference and
  release rules covering install, carry-over, failed builds, and old-table
  reclamation; alternatively use a validated stable volume identity. No
  ownership change was made here; this needs a dedicated design and rereview.
- A no-trigger failure before volume enumeration now uses the global
  protected-name quarantine until retry proves coverage; a late-attach trigger
  still scopes this case to its supported fixed NTFS volume. This protects
  protected-name opens without denying paging writes or names outside policy.
- The focused `Test-StagedLateAttachUnload.ps1 -FailurePath` now records the
  exact 65 writable mappings versus the 64-entry bound, confirms quarantine,
  tests a writable `%TEMP%` mapping on C: outside configured scope, disposes all
  mappings, observes the exact marker through aligned unbuffered raw-volume
  reads while quarantined and after automatic retry, requires recovery while
  still attached, then unloads and cleans up. It emits separate
  `QuarantineLifecycle=PASS` and `PrivacyExposure=OBSERVED|NOT_OBSERVED` fields;
  a public marker emits `KNOWN_PRIVACY_GAP` and exit 3, while no observation is
  exit 4 and is not a privacy pass. This is harness source only; it has not run.
- The six run-18 PREfast findings have corresponding source changes: typed DPC
  routine declaration/definition, pageable refresh placement and IRQL
  annotations, isolated spin-lock helpers, non-NULL worker-context guard, and
  checked FltObjectReference status. The run-18 log remains the last build
  evidence. These changes are not verified until the builder reruns WDK
  PREfast.
- Normalized-name checks against the installed name table remain at both
  create gates; publication rename still refuses a quarantined target volume.

Cutover status: BLOCKED. The teardown admission race, attach-to-scan write
window, and 65/64 paging-write exposure remain open. No clean review, build, or

Round-4 source follow-up (2026-10-03; awaiting independent reread/build)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Fixed the supported-volume setup queue-failure regression. `InstanceSetup`
  reserves a nonblocking callback admission through its return; its refresh
  work has a separate reservation. Volume classification errors, allocation,
  reference, queue, or retryable reservation failures record a per-volume
  protected-name quarantine (or global fallback) and retain the ordinary
  SafeUpload attachment; positively unsupported volumes stay attached and are
  skipped. An accepted setup during CLOSING is drained or leaves state
  that vetoes voluntary unload. The gate becomes CLOSED only at the final
  atomic commit after all fallible Stage drains; only a setup arriving after
  that commit may return DO_NOT_ATTACH. No documented callback-serialization
  assumption is used. Microsoft documents that InstanceSetup errors prevent
  attachment and warns against thread synchronization/IPC in the callback;
  FilterUnload's success return is the unload decision:
  https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows-hardware/drivers/ddi/fltkernel/nc-fltkernel-pflt_instance_setup_callback
  https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows-hardware/drivers/ddi/fltkernel/nc-fltkernel-pflt_filter_unload_callback
- Removed quarantine pruning based on absence from one mutable
  `FltEnumerateVolumes` result. Scans now append remembered quarantined
  identities with independent references and cover them alongside the current
  enumeration; a quarantine-generation check prevents clearing state added
  after the remembered set was copied. Global quarantine is also retained while
  InstanceSetup callbacks or late-trigger scans are in flight. A global fallback
  from an unremembered identity or full quarantine table is sticky through retry
  and unload; its
  protected-name refusal can remain global until mandatory filter teardown
  because the voluntary unload guard refuses while quarantine remains and a
  later snapshot cannot prove the absent identity was removed. This fail-closed
  capacity fallback may require a system restart. It does not deny paging writes
  or names outside protected scope.
- CLOSED-gate attach refusal is after the atomic final commit. No later branch
  in voluntary unload returns DO_NOT_DETACH; before commit, callback admission
  or quarantined state forces a veto. This is an explicit source protocol,
  not a claim that Filter Manager serializes InstanceSetup with FilterUnload.
- Still unresolved and cutover-blocking: QueryTeardown remains point-in-time;
  an attach/scan may start after it returns and before teardown completes.
  The attachment-to-scan paging-write interval remains open because InstanceSetup
  must not wait. The 65/64 partial-table discard still exposes mapped paging
  writes; the harness must report a public marker as `KNOWN_PRIVACY_GAP`.
  The borrowed raw `PFLT_VOLUME` identities in the installed table remain a
  P2 lifetime concern. The six run-18 PREfast fixes are source-only pending a
  fresh WDK analysis. No clean review/build/VM result is claimed.

Round-5 source follow-up (2026-10-03; source candidate awaiting reread)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
- StageAdmit now checks exact normalized names in the installed fence table
  before current-policy out-of-scope and service/publication paths. The
  writable-section callback checks installed SOP identity and exact carried
  names before current/pending policy, removes its blanket Inspector bypass,
  and denies unresolved names. Publication rename continues to reject a
  quarantined target volume.
- Candidate transition scans reject candidate prefixes with skipped or
  unavailable coverage, requested network/removable scope, and reparses in a
  required candidate scope. The Inspector complete predicate now includes
  reparses and unresolved section/FSCTL state. The unload scan/commit also
  refuses skipped reparses.
- Unknown attached volume kinds, including missing instance-context fallback,
  conservatively match configured network/removable policy flags and explicit
  prefixes in the feature build. For Unknown instances, create/write
  name-query failures remain monitored even under prefix-only policy, and a
  rename-destination query failure is refused as monitored. An unresolved
  name never grants a user override. Known fixed-volume name-query failures
  retain the existing policy behavior. Context installation failure is traced
  and retains ordinary callbacks. Positively unsupported volumes
  remain attached, and normal-build classification behavior is preserved.
  This fallback has static assertions only; no Windows fault injection has
  exercised allocation/context-install failure.
- Feature-build QueryTeardown unconditionally refuses manual detaches. This
  removes the manual check-then-act window. Microsoft documents the callback
  as manual-detach-only; mandatory unload and volume dismount do not invoke it
  and remain non-vetoable.
- The installed stream table acquires a Filter Manager reference for each
  stored `PFLT_VOLUME` association and releases it on failed-build/table
  reclamation. This source-level ownership change addresses the stale-pointer
  P2 but has not had independent rereview.
- Trigger presence is now proved against entries marked as returned by the
  exact `FltEnumerateVolumes` call; remembered quarantined identities appended
  later cannot satisfy that proof. The mutable enumeration remains a
  snapshot, so late-attach pre-scan exposure is still open.
- Setup admission still ends immediately before the callback returns; no
  documented post-return acceptance signal was found. Keep this unload edge
  open. Filter Manager calls InstanceSetup on the first operation after mount
  and forbids callback synchronization/IPC; under the no-volume-wide-paging-
  denial rule, the async attach-to-scan window cannot be closed here. Require
  an external pre-handle/section admission gate for every enabled volume or
  leave staging off.
- A 65-stream failure still discards the partial table; quarantine does not
  block paging writes missing from the table. The byte-privacy objective stays
  BLOCKED. Sticky quarantine can persist to mandatory filter teardown or a
  system restart. The old same-volume attach harness now explicitly reports
  `NOT_RUN_MANUAL_DETACH_DISABLED` and exit 5 for this candidate; it does not
  claim a late-attach/overflow pass. The policy-transition harness keeps its
  original expansion repro and adds a shrink check for fresh-reader and
  preexisting-handle writable-section refusal plus an independent raw-volume
  marker read. Failed mapping disposal makes the measurement inconclusive; an
  observed marker is a privacy failure. Both scenarios are source-only and
  unrun.
- Destination blockers remain: managed USB automount/access proof; an
  always-present early MUP filter for UNC/SMB (not implemented); and the fact
  that pausing/disconnecting a sync client does not drain existing handles or
  mappings. No DOD gate or full product objective is complete.
